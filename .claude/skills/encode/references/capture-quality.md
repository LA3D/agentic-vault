# Capture quality

Shared criteria for Encode and Review Note. Apply to the requested capture scope;
explicit raw-only preservation does not promise synthesis.

## Recoverable knowledge

Treat maintained Markdown as durable, queryable memory. A future agent using
these notes without reopening the source or a binary attachment should recover
project-relevant exact quantities with units, named systems/models/datasets/methods,
conditions, material qualifications, exceptions, and evidence status. Retain them
in labeled sections, tables, or linked maintained notes. Be selective: preserve
what bears on the purpose and likely future questions, not every detail. A concise
opening is useful; a summary that loses decisive particulars fails.

Keep provenance and distinguish source claims from interpretation. Do not turn
opinion, projection, or qualified results into established fact. State incomplete
source access and what could not be checked. A binary attachment or unintegrated
raw archive alone does not satisfy a request to incorporate knowledge. Never
alter a preserved original to make the maintained account appear better supported.

## Published-author identity

For published source capture, resolve bylines against existing `author-note`
coverage, aliases and source evidence. Reuse verified identities through the
canonical `author:` edge where its domain/range permits, retaining display
`authors:` metadata. An author/thinker note indexes published work and intellectual
contributions; it is distinct from a personal-contact `person` note. Do not infer contact details, acquaintance, affiliation or biography from authorship.

Create a new author note selectively when explicitly requested or when recurring
coverage establishes a clear, source-grounded need within the capture scope.
Do not create one for every byline. Leave ambiguous identity unasserted and report
what remains uncertain. Index new author notes appropriately and add reciprocal
navigation to captured works, distinguishing their authored works from vault
syntheses discussing their ideas.

## Conceptual integration

Read the source's substantive ideas, search existing coverage using those ideas
and alternative phrasing, and inspect plausible concept/theory/method notes.
Titles and keyword hits alone do not establish relationships. Check relevant
neighbors for supporting, extending, or opposing claims.

Attach justified `concept:` edges for concepts actually discussed, using canonical
vocabulary domain/range. Use another valid edge or body link for useful targets
of other types; do not relabel a reference as a concept to pass a checklist.
Preserve qualifiers and direction. A parent/index entry does not replace
applicable conceptual relationships.

If a useful concept is missing, create it only when source-supported and within
the requested integration scope; otherwise report the coverage gap. There is no
required number of links or companion pages. Reuse adequate existing notes.
If no conceptual edge is justified, report searched scope and reason rather than
inventing one. Assess this from content even when `concept:` is absent: traversing
only supplied edges cannot detect omitted relationships.

Make maintained notes reachable through their index and useful concept
back-references. Distinguish original and maintained interpretation when both
exist. Already-complete integration may need no edits.

## Evidence of completion

Read saved Markdown and test representative future questions: can relevant
particulars and qualifications be answered, and can a reader reach the account
from its concepts? Inspect candidate neighbors independently of the writer's
selected links. Verify newly asserted links and types. Valid YAML or tidy prose
alone is insufficient.

Report confirmed omissions, unsupported relationships, source drift, and access
limits with evidence. Fix material failures before claiming capture completion.
Draft and batch review may omit cosmetic polish, but not recoverability or
conceptual integration for substantive capture.
